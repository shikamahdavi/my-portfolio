Executive summary

Make good judgment repeatable before volume arrives

The team is strong, focused, and early in a new model. My role would be to make its decision logic visible, help skilled practitioners consult with more confidence, and build the conditions for learning to scale without adding people or static content.

Consistency over a single entry point

Requests can come from Rachel, the team, Line HR, business leaders, managers, or Enterprise Learning. Whoever receives one uses the same discovery questions, decision guardrails, and prioritization logic.

Consulting before content

Every request is treated as a business problem first. The team decides whether the answer is learning, coaching, process, technology, manager reinforcement, performance support, or a combination. Training is one option, not the default.

Lead the people, not only the process

Build on each person's craft expertise. Model discovery on real requests, review decisions together, and widen autonomy within clear guardrails.

Scale through the flow of work

Smaller modular assets, performance support, manager-owned reinforcement, and explicit content ownership reduce maintenance and keep learning current.

Pilot, measure, then scale

Test the model on one real request by Day 90. Measure time to root cause, handoffs, stakeholder experience, and decision consistency before broader rollout.

What I heard

Drawn only from my conversations with Rachel and Jeff. Select a theme to see what I take it to mean for the role.

A strong team that needs a different way of working

People can enter through any door. The experience once they enter should be consistent.

My paraphrase of Rachel's point on intake.

  • Small team, strong in traditional learning craft: an implementation specialist, a learning experience designer, and a learning experience architect.
  • Move away from being order takers. Get to root cause faster and reduce unnecessary handoffs.
  • Decide whether the real answer is training, coaching, process, technology, or something else.
  • Avoid one person saying yes to everything while another says no to similar requests.
  • Focus on the highest-impact priorities: AI, product management, Fidelity Digital Assets, the digital organization, and UX / user research adjacency.
  • Still early in the model, not yet overwhelmed with requests. The right time to build the way of working.
  • Interested in how AI might make intake faster and more structured.
  • Needs someone who leads people, not only someone who builds a process.

The model for learning is still being figured out

Common fundamentals, without one rigid model for every business.

My paraphrase of Jeff's view across Fidelity.

  • Scale learning and move away from facilitator-led training for everything.
  • Embed learning in the flow of work and in manager practices.
  • Design for how employees consume information today. Reduce static content that goes stale quickly.
  • Leaders often learned in traditional ways and may expect workshops or modules.
  • Brokerage is younger, built its talent and learning infrastructure from the ground up, can move faster, and uses fewer learning professionals.
  • More emphasis on consulting with leaders and helping managers own part of the experience.
  • Rachel's work lands because it is practical, makes sense to business leaders, and connects to what the business is trying to do.

The same direction from two vantage points

Consult with leaders. Solve the right problem. Let it scale through the work.
  • Consulting over producing. Rachel wants the team to stop taking orders. Jeff wants learning to consult with leaders instead of producing content.
  • Scale with a small team. Rachel's team is three people. Jeff noted Brokerage uses fewer learning professionals by design.
  • Practical, business-anchored work. Jeff credits Rachel's work for this. The model has to protect it.
  • Flexibility within shared principles. Rachel rejects a single front door. Jeff rejects one rigid learning model. Both point to common standards with room to adapt.

Modern intake model

Any Door. One Consistent Experience.

No forced front door. Requests arrive where relationships already exist. What stays constant is what happens next. Select an entry point to trace it.

Shared consulting logic
Same discovery questions8 core questions
Same root-cause lensLearning or not?
Same prioritization logicTransparent rubric
Same decision guardrailsWho decides, who escalates
Consistent stakeholder experienceSame follow-through

Without shared logic

  • One team member says yes to a course request. Another says no to a similar one.
  • The requester's experience depends on who they happened to call.
  • Requests get passed along before anyone defines the problem.
  • Priority goes to whoever asks loudest or first.

With shared logic

  • Whoever receives the request runs the same discovery and owns it within guardrails.
  • Similar requests get similar answers, with reasons the requester can see.
  • Fewer handoffs, because the first person can diagnose.
  • Rachel can trust the standard without approving every decision.
